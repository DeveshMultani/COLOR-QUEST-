## 🧠 Color Theory Concepts

### RGB — Additive Color Model

RGB represents colors using red, green, and blue light.

It is commonly used in digital displays. Combining the three
channels at maximum intensity produces white in the standard
RGB model.

### CMYK — Subtractive Color Model

CMYK represents cyan, magenta, yellow, and black ink channels.

It is primarily associated with printing, where inks absorb
portions of the light reflected by the printed surface.

RGB and CMYK have different characteristics and color gamuts.
A generic conversion does not guarantee printer-specific
color accuracy.

### HSV — Hue, Saturation, and Value

HSV describes colors through three properties:

- **Hue:** The color's position around the color wheel.
- **Saturation:** The intensity or purity of the color.
- **Value:** The brightness component in the HSV representation.

### Color Schemes

The project is designed around five fundamental color schemes:

1. **Monochromatic:** Variations of a single hue.
2. **Complementary:** Colors positioned opposite one another
   on the color wheel.
3. **Analogous:** Colors positioned near one another on the
   color wheel.
4. **Warm:** Colors generally associated with warmth, including
   reds, oranges, and yellows.
5. **Cool:** Colors generally associated with coolness, including
   blues, greens, and blue-violets.
